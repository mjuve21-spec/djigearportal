Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to register my drone with the FAA?

In the United States, drones weighing over 250g (0.55 lbs) require FAA registration for recreational use. The Mini 3, Mini 4 Pro, Mini 5 Pro, and Neo all weigh 249g or less to avoid this requirement. The Air 3S, Mavic 4 Pro, Flip, and Inspire 3 all require registration, which costs $5 and is valid for 3 years.

What's the difference between Mavic 4 Pro and Air 3S?

The Mavic 4 Pro offers superior image quality with 5.1K recording and a Hasselblad camera, better low-light performance, and more advanced features. The Air 3S has longer battery life (45 vs 43 minutes), dual cameras for versatility, and costs $650 less. For most content creators, the Air 3S offers better value. Choose Mavic 4 Pro only if you need the absolute best image quality for professional work.

Can I fly my drone anywhere?

No. There are restricted areas including near airports (within 5 miles), over people, above 400 feet altitude, in national parks, and certain urban areas. DJI drones have built-in geofencing that prevents flight in restricted zones. Always check the B4UFLY app (US) or local regulations before flying. You must keep the drone in visual line of sight at all times.

Which drone has the best camera?

For absolute image quality: Inspire 3 with 8K RAW. For consumer drones: Mavic 4 Pro with 5.1K Hasselblad camera. For best value: Air 3S with dual 4K cameras. For portability: Mini 5 Pro with 4K/60fps. The "best" camera depends on your specific needs - resolution isn't everything. Sensor size, low-light performance, and features matter too.

How long do batteries really last?

Advertised flight times are measured in perfect conditions (no wind, hovering only). Real-world flight times with active flying and recording are typically 60-75% of advertised times. Air 3S: ~30-35 minutes real-world. Mavic 4 Pro: ~28-33 minutes. Mini series: ~22-28 minutes. Always land with 15-20% battery remaining as a safety buffer.

Do I need a license to fly commercially?

Yes. In the US, you need an FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate to fly drones for any commercial purpose (real estate, weddings, inspections, paid social media content, etc.). The test costs $175 and covers airspace, weather, regulations, and safety. Study materials are available online. For recreational flying only, no special license is needed beyond following TRUST training.
