Key Differences That Actually Matter

Sensor Size Impact

Sensor size directly correlates to image quality, especially in low light. The Mavic 4 Pro's 1-inch sensor captures significantly more light than the Mini 5's 1/2.3-inch sensor, resulting in cleaner footage at dusk and dawn. If you shoot golden hour or interiors, sensor size matters enormously.

Obstacle Avoidance Systems

The Mavic 4 Pro has omnidirectional sensing (front, back, sides, top, bottom), while budget models only have 3-direction sensing. In complex environments like forests or urban areas, comprehensive obstacle avoidance can be the difference between a safe flight and a crashed drone. For beginners especially, better sensors = fewer accidents.

Battery Life Reality

Advertised flight times are measured in perfect conditions (no wind, hovering). Real-world flight times are typically 60-75% of advertised. The Air 4's 46-minute rating translates to about 30-35 minutes of actual flying and recording. Always land with 15-20% battery remaining as a safety buffer.

Weight Class Regulations

Drones under 250g (Mini 5, Mini 5 Pro) don't require FAA registration in the US. This saves $5 and the hassle of registration, plus these drones face fewer restrictions in some areas. However, lighter drones are more affected by wind. Consider your typical flying conditions when choosing weight class.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to register my drone with the FAA?

In the United States, any drone weighing over 250g (0.55 lbs) requires FAA registration for recreational use. The DJI Mini 5 and Mini 5 Pro both weigh exactly 249g to stay under this threshold. Registration costs $5, is valid for 3 years, and can be done online in about 5 minutes. You need to be at least 13 years old to register.

What's the difference between Mavic 4 Pro and Air 4?

The Mavic 4 Pro has a larger 1-inch sensor (vs 1/1.3"), records in 5.1K (vs 4K), includes omnidirectional obstacle sensing (vs 4-direction), has 1TB internal storage (vs 256GB), and offers variable aperture control. The Air 4 costs $1,100 less and actually has longer battery life (46 vs 43 minutes) and better range (18km vs 15km). For most users, the Air 4 is the better value. Get the Mavic 4 Pro only if you need the absolute best image quality for professional work.

Can I fly my drone anywhere?

No, there are restricted areas including near airports, over people, above 400 feet, in national parks, and in certain urban areas. DJI drones have built-in geofencing that prevents flight in restricted zones. Always check the B4UFLY app (US) or local regulations before flying. Most countries require keeping the drone in visual line of sight.

How far can I legally fly?

In the US, FAA rules require keeping your drone within visual line of sight (VLOS) at all times, typically about 0.5 miles maximum. While drones like the Air 4 have 18km transmission range, you cannot legally fly that far. You must be able to see your drone with the naked eye (not through FPV goggles or screen) throughout the entire flight.

Are DJI drones waterproof?

No, DJI consumer drones (Mini, Air, Mavic series) are not waterproof or water-resistant. Flying in rain, snow, fog, or even high humidity can damage the electronics and motors. Only fly in dry conditions. The exception is the DJI Matrice series (industrial/enterprise drones) which have IP ratings for weather resistance.

Do I need a license to fly commercially?

Yes, in the US you need an FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate to fly drones commercially (real estate, weddings, inspections, any paid work). The test covers airspace, weather, regulations, and safety. Study materials are widely available online and the test costs $175. For recreational flying only, no special license is needed beyond following TRUST training and local regulations.

What accessories do I need?

Essential: Extra batteries ($100-200 each, get at least 2-3 total), microSD cards (at least 128GB), propeller guards for beginners. Highly recommended: ND filters for video ($50-100), carrying case, landing pad. Nice to have: Tablet mount, extra props, car charger. Budget an extra 30-40% beyond the drone's base price for necessary accessories.

Which drone has the best camera?

For image quality, the Inspire 4 is the clear winner with its 8K full-frame sensor. For consumer drones, the Mavic 4 Pro has the best camera with its 1-inch sensor and 5.1K recording. For value, the Air 4 offers excellent 4K/120fps quality at a much lower price. For portability with good quality, the Mini 5 Pro punches above its weight with 4K/60fps in a tiny package.
