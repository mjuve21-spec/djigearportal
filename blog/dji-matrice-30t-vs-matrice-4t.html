DJI Matrice 30T vs Matrice 4T:
Which Should Your Team Buy?

The Matrice 4T is newer, cheaper, and has better specs on paper. But the Matrice 30T still wins in specific situations. Here's the honest breakdown.

By DJI Gear Portal  ·  Updated June 2026

Quick verdict For most new buyers in 2026, the Matrice 4T is the better choice — it costs roughly $2,000 less, has a better camera system, longer flight time, and a superior laser rangefinder. The Matrice 30T is still the right call if you need IP55 waterproofing in truly harsh conditions, are expanding an existing M30 fleet, or have mission-critical reliability requirements where proven track record matters more than newer specs.

These are serious professional tools — not consumer drones. Both are used by public safety agencies, utility companies, construction firms, and inspection teams. A single sale can generate a meaningful commission, and more importantly, buyers in this category are doing real research before spending $8,000–$10,000. This page gives them the honest information they need.

Which One Is Right for Your Team?

Choose the Matrice 4T if…

  • You're buying new and don't already own M30 equipment
  • Budget matters — $2,000 savings is real money
  • You need the 1,800m rangefinder for long-range work
  • You want full-colour night mode for public safety or SAR
  • Longer flight time (49 vs 41 min) matters for your missions
  • You want hot-swappable payload flexibility
  • You're connecting to DJI Dock 3 for autonomous ops

Choose the Matrice 30T if…

  • You already operate an M30 fleet and want consistency
  • Your missions involve heavy rain or submersion risk (IP55)
  • You fly in extreme temperatures below -20°C regularly
  • Ultra-fast 1-minute deployment is critical (SAR, fire)
  • Your team is trained on M30 workflows
  • You need the established M30 accessory ecosystem
Honest take: The Matrice 4T wins on almost every spec at a lower price. The Matrice 30T's main advantages in 2026 are its IP55 rating (vs IP54 on the 4T), its ultra-fast deployment, and its established track record with teams that already use it. For most new buyers, the 4T is the smarter investment.

The Differences That Actually Matter

Camera Quality — 4T Wins Clearly

The most important practical difference for inspection work is the zoom camera. The Matrice 30T has a 12MP zoom; the Matrice 4T has a 48MP zoom — four times the resolution. When you're reading serial numbers on equipment, inspecting weld seams, or identifying structural defects from altitude, that difference is not subtle. The 4T also gets the newer FLIR Boson+ thermal sensor, which detects smaller temperature differences — useful for early hot spot detection on solar panels or finding heat signatures in search and rescue.

Laser Rangefinder — 4T Wins

The Matrice 4T's laser rangefinder reaches 1,800m; the 30T's reaches 1,200m. For utility inspection, infrastructure work, or public safety operations where you're identifying targets at distance, 600 extra meters of usable range is genuinely meaningful.

IP Rating — 30T Wins (Just)

IP55 vs IP54 is a relatively small difference on paper — both protect against dust and water jets. The M30T's IP55 rating offers slightly more protection against sustained water exposure. For most inspection and public safety missions, IP54 is adequate. If your team regularly operates in driving rain, on boats, or near water surfaces, the M30T's extra protection is worth noting.

Flight Time — 4T Wins

49 minutes vs 41 minutes is a meaningful edge in the field. Eight extra minutes per battery cycle adds up across a full day of operations, and it reduces how often you're swapping batteries during time-sensitive missions.

Weight and Portability

The Matrice 4T weighs 1,570g — less than half the Matrice 30T's 3,770g. For one-person teams or rapid deployment scenarios, that difference affects how quickly you can move between locations. The M30T's weight does give it an advantage in stability in high winds with payloads, but both drones share Level 6 wind resistance ratings.

Commercial Use — Part 107 Required

Important for US operators: Both drones are enterprise tools intended for commercial use. In the US, commercial drone operations require a FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot Certificate regardless of drone weight. If your team doesn't yet have Part 107 certification, you'll need it before flying either of these commercially. Training resources are available from providers like Drone Pilot Ground School.
