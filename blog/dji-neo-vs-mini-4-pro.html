DJI Neo vs Mini 4 Pro:
$149 or $759 — Which Is Right for You?

The Neo is DJI's cheapest drone. The Mini 4 Pro is five times the price. But they're not really competing — they're built for completely different people.

By DJI Gear Portal  ·  Updated June 2026

Quick verdict Get the Neo if you're a complete beginner, want a fun selfie drone, or just want to try flying without spending much. Get the Mini 4 Pro if you care about image quality, want to shoot serious footage, or plan to fly regularly. These two drones aren't really head-to-head — they serve different needs at very different prices.

The DJI Neo launched at $199 and has since dropped to $149 — making it DJI's most affordable camera drone ever. The Mini 4 Pro sits at $759 and is aimed at serious hobbyists and content creators. Most people cross-shopping these two are asking a simple question: is the Mini 4 Pro really worth five times more? The honest answer: it depends entirely on what you want to do with a drone.

Which One Is Right for You?

Choose the Neo if…

  • You've never flown a drone before
  • You want quick selfies and social media clips
  • Budget is the main factor
  • You want to fly indoors or in tight spaces
  • You don't want to carry a controller
  • You want to try FPV flying cheaply first
  • You're buying as a gift for a young person

Choose the Mini 4 Pro if…

  • You care about image quality
  • You shoot YouTube, Instagram, or client work
  • You want 4K/60fps or slow motion footage
  • You fly outdoors regularly in varied conditions
  • You need longer flight time per battery
  • You want omnidirectional obstacle avoidance
  • You're serious about drone photography
Honest take: These two drones aren't really competing. The Neo is a beginner fun-flyer and selfie drone. The Mini 4 Pro is a serious camera tool. If you're asking "which takes better footage" — the Mini 4 Pro wins easily. If you're asking "which should I start with as a complete beginner on a budget" — the Neo makes a lot of sense at $149.

The Differences That Actually Matter

Image Quality — Mini 4 Pro Wins Clearly

The Mini 4 Pro's 1/1.3-inch sensor is significantly larger than the Neo's 1/2-inch sensor — that means better low-light performance, more detail, and cleaner footage at higher ISO. The Neo's 4K/30fps footage looks good on a phone screen; on a TV or in editing, the gap is obvious. If you're creating content for an audience, the Mini 4 Pro is the better tool.

Ease of Use — Neo Wins

The Neo can launch from your palm with no controller at all — just the DJI Fly app or even gesture control. It has built-in propeller guards and a simple button on top to activate flight modes. It's genuinely the easiest drone DJI has ever made. The Mini 4 Pro requires a controller, a bit of practice, and more setup — but it's still very beginner-friendly compared to most drones.

Flight Time — Mini 4 Pro Wins Significantly

18 minutes vs 34 minutes is a big difference. In real-world use, the Neo typically gives you 13–14 minutes per battery. The Mini 4 Pro gives you 25–30 minutes. If you're shooting any kind of extended footage, you'll be swapping Neo batteries constantly. Extra Neo batteries are cheap at least.

Built-in Storage — Neo Wins (Surprisingly)

The Neo has 22GB built-in. The Mini 4 Pro has only 2GB and almost always needs a microSD card. A small thing, but the Neo actually beats the Mini 4 Pro on this one detail.

FPV Flying

Both drones support FPV-style flying with goggles — but they use different systems. The Neo works with DJI Goggles N3 and RC Motion 3 for an accessible FPV experience. The Mini 4 Pro works with DJI Goggles 3. The Neo is actually a more natural entry point into FPV flying thanks to its simpler controls and lower stakes if you crash.

A Note on the Neo 2

DJI has since released the Neo 2, which adds omnidirectional obstacle avoidance and a foldable design. If you're buying new and the Neo 2 is available at B&H, it's worth considering over the original Neo. Your B&H link above goes to the original Neo — check availability for the Neo 2 separately.
