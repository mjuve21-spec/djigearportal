Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to register my DJI drone with the FAA?

In the United States, any drone weighing over 250g requires FAA registration if flying recreationally. The Mini 5 and Mini 5 Pro weigh exactly 249g to stay under this threshold. Registration costs $5 and is valid for 3 years.

What's the difference between Mavic 4 Pro and Air 4?

The Mavic 4 Pro features a larger 1-inch sensor (vs 1/1.3"), records in 5.1K (vs 4K), includes omnidirectional obstacle sensing, has 1TB internal storage, and delivers superior low-light performance. The Air 4 offers better battery life and longer range at nearly half the price.

Can I fly DJI drones in rain or snow?

No, DJI consumer drones are not waterproof or water-resistant. Flying in rain, snow, or fog can damage electronics and motors. Only fly in dry conditions. The exception is the DJI Matrice series (industrial drones) which have weather resistance ratings.

How far can I legally fly my drone?

In the US, FAA rules require keeping your drone within visual line of sight at all times, typically about 0.5 miles maximum. While drones like the Air 4 have 18km range, you cannot legally fly that far. You must see your drone with the naked eye throughout the flight.

What's the real-world flight time?

Advertised flight times are maximum theoretical times in ideal conditions. Real-world flight times are typically 60-75% of advertised times. The Mavic 4 Pro's 43-minute rating translates to about 30-35 minutes of actual flight time. Always land with 15-20% battery remaining.

Which drone is best for absolute beginners?

The DJI Mini 5 is the best beginner drone at $489. It's affordable, has intuitive controls, helpful beginner modes, and decent obstacle avoidance. The 249g design means no FAA registration hassle. Once you master the basics, you can upgrade to Air 4 or Mavic 4 Pro.
